# Consumer Context Gate | v0.4A

Purpose: connect a product, design, or experience question to the relevant **consumer decision**, without inventing market behavior. This is a context *selection* guide, not a fixed long questionnaire.

## Trigger
Use when the decision concerns consumers, product adoption, purchase, usage, retention, support, reputation, or entry into a new geographic market. Skip broad lifecycle analysis for a bounded UI detail unless the user's decision depends on it.

## Consumer decision context (select only what matters)
1. **Decision and stage**: discovery, comparison, purchase, onboarding, regular use, repair, exit, advocacy or complaint; identify the actual choice in dispute.
2. **Actors**: buyer, payer, primary/secondary user, recommender, installer, caregiver, maintainer, return-handler. Different roles may have different goals. Do not assume they are identical.
3. **Category and substitutes**: proposed product, current workarounds, alternatives; clarify whether proposed 'need' is a preselected solution.
4. **Use and ownership economics**: learning, setup, maintenance, consumables, service, and recovery costs if decision-relevant. Do not conflate cost to user with company ROI.
5. **Channels, communication, expectations**: discovery, sales promise, unboxing, customer service and public reaction when relevant. Study claim-vs-experience mismatch as evidence for product/communication corrections, not a mandate to change PR messaging.
6. **Scope**: country/region, urban/rural or demographic segment where relevant; never assume 'East'/'West' is one consumer type.

## Minimal output
- Decision at stake: who can choose what now?
- Relevant actors / journey stage(s): not a generic persona deck.
- Observed facts / supplied market assumptions / professional inferences clearly separated.
- 1–3 user-facing implications that change a product or research choice; include an alternative explanation or counterexample for consequential findings.
- Decision effect: act now / conditionally select / compare / narrow / postpone; state what would reverse it.

If the user provides no actual consumer study, output a **conditional preassessment**; do not claim verified preference, conversion, adoption, or demand size. If user facts cannot distinguish buyer and user, keep both possible until shown otherwise.
