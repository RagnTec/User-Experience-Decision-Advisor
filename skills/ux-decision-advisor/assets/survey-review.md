## Review rules

- One item measures one construct.
- Use neutral wording and balanced scales.
- Single-select options must be mutually exclusive when the question truly requires one answer.
- Coexisting behaviors must be split, anchored to one event, or measured with multi-select plus a separate importance/recency item.
- Include “not applicable,” “do not know,” and refusal/skip paths where needed.
- Every option must route to a valid next item or end.
- Define recall window and exposure eligibility.
- Pretest comprehension, routing, and completion burden.
- Keep self-report distinct from observed behavior.

## Example targeted revision

**Original:** “Which best describes your use of the feature?”  
Options: noticed it / used it / ignored it / disliked it.

**Defect:** noticing, using, ignoring, and evaluation can coexist and mix behavior with attitude.

**Revision:**

- Q1, single select, last 7 days: “Which best describes your most recent contact with the feature?”  
  A. Used it to complete a task  
  B. Opened it but did not complete a task  
  C. Saw it but did not open it because I did not need it  
  D. Needed the function but did not find or understand the entry  
  E. Did not encounter the relevant page/task  
  F. Do not remember
- Q2, only if Q1=A or B, multi-select: “Which of the following happened during that contact?”  
  [task-specific behavior options]  
  “None of these” must be exclusive or handled in software.
- Q3, only if relevant: “How difficult or easy was it to [specific task]?” [balanced scale + not applicable/do not know]
- Q4, optional open text: “What happened at the point you found most difficult or unclear?”

State the denominator for each branch and do not interpret Q1=D as “the entry was not visible.” It is a self-reported reason.
